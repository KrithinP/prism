\begin{abstract}
\pending{PENDING -- written last.}
We study \emph{where} and \emph{how} to fuse RGB and thermal features inside a single-stage
detector, and --- unlike prior work that reports on geometrically aligned benchmarks --- we
measure how each design choice behaves when the thermal stream is spatially misaligned,
temporally stale, or absent. On the KAIST multispectral pedestrian benchmark we hold the
detector (YOLOX), training schedule, and augmentation fixed and vary only the fusion stage and
operator, reporting log-average miss rate (MR$^{-2}$) with seed variance. \pending{headline
results PENDING.} Our central questions: does the fusion operator matter on aligned data; which
operators degrade gracefully under misalignment; and can a fusion detector remain
\emph{composable} --- as good as its RGB-only baseline when thermal is missing, and better when
it is present.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Multispectral pedestrian detection combines a visible (RGB) and a long-wave infrared (thermal)
camera because the two are complementary: RGB carries colour and texture and fails at night;
thermal images emitted heat and sees people in darkness but cannot see through glass and washes
out during thermal crossover. The dominant design is \emph{middle fusion}: two modality-specific
backbones whose features are merged partway through the network.

Two gaps motivate this work. First, on the standard aligned benchmark the reported gap between
cheap and expensive fusion operators is small, while data-quality factors dominate --- suggesting
the operator may matter far less than the literature's architectural emphasis implies. Second,
real dual-camera rigs are not aligned or synchronised: fields of view differ, parallax shifts
objects, and a thermal frame may arrive late or not at all. A fusion detector that is excellent
on aligned data but collapses when thermal is stale or missing is not deployable.

We therefore run a \emph{controlled} study: one detector, one schedule, one augmentation policy,
varying only the fusion design, and we make sensor degradation a first-class experimental axis.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Base detector}
We use YOLOX: a CSPDarknet backbone emitting features at strides 8/16/32, a PAFPN neck, and an
anchor-free decoupled head with SimOTA label assignment. Label assignment and losses are held
fixed across all experiments; they are not ablated. \pending{cite YOLOX.}

\subsection{Two-stream fusion}
RGB and thermal are processed by independent CSPDarknet backbones. Their features are merged at
one or more stages (dark3/dark4/dark5) by a fusion operator that returns tensors of the original
shape, so the neck and head are unchanged. \pending{final architecture figure PENDING.}

\subsection{Ablation axes}
\emph{(A) Fusion stage:} dark3 / dark4 / dark5 / all.
\emph{(B) Fusion operator:} element-wise sum, max; concatenation$+1\times1$; learned-scalar;
channel (SE) gating; illumination gating; and a cross-attention ceiling reference.
\emph{(C) Weight sharing} between streams.
\emph{(D) Misalignment:} injected spatial shift and temporal skew.
\emph{(E) Composability:} random per-frame thermal availability at train time; evaluation with
thermal present / absent / stale. Every operator accepts a missing stream via a validity flag.

\subsection{Evaluation}
We report the standard KAIST metric suite, primary being log-average miss rate MR$^{-2}$ over
FPPI $\in[10^{-2},10^0]$ on the Reasonable subset (height $\geq 55$px, occlusion $\leq$ partial),
split All / Day / Night, using sanitized-train and improved-test annotations. Secondary: the
All-condition setting and the scale/occlusion subset breakdowns of the Caltech protocol; mAP is
included only as a cross-check and no claim rests on it (mAP masked a modality-collapsed fusion in
prior in-house work). Our evaluator is validated against a published method's released detections
\pending{verification PENDING}. Every result is mean$\pm$std over \textbf{5 seeds}; a difference
below 2$\sigma$ is not treated as a result.

\section{Experimental Setup}
KAIST: 95{,}324 aligned RGB--thermal pairs. Training uses the standard every-2nd-frame list of
sets 00--05 (25{,}086 frames, of which 9{,}288 contain a pedestrian) with sanitized annotations;
the whole set contains only $\sim$1{,}182 unique pedestrians. Test is the standard 2{,}252-image
split (1{,}455 day / 797 night) with improved annotations. Hardware: single RTX 5070 Ti (12\,GB).

Given a small model ($8.94$\,M parameters) and a small dataset, all models are \textbf{trained
from scratch} (no ImageNet/COCO pretraining), so every configuration starts identically and no
pretrained-weight prior confounds the comparison; the trade-off is higher absolute MR$^{-2}$ than
pretrained methods in the literature, which is immaterial to a study of \emph{deltas} between
fusion designs. Learning rate and decay are set by a small sweep before the recipe is locked; the
recipe is then held fixed across all experiments. \pending{final optimiser, LR, epochs, batch
